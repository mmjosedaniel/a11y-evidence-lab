Work must follow the derived [development roadmap](../DEVELOPMENT_ROADMAP.md) through concrete user-requested tasks. The first implementation scope remains one vertical portfolio slice, not six independently generalized subsystems:

1. Freeze the product scope, the three controlled scenario profiles, and their expected deterministic scanner outcomes before implementation. Freeze the exact evaluation inputs, expected retrieved passages, and scoring rubric before inspecting model output; implementation literals that do not affect those outcomes may be chosen during development.
2. Implement one reusable closed loop that accepts one trusted developer/operator-supplied public HTTPS page, runs one complete deterministic scan limited to `image-alt`, `label`, and `color-contrast`, and retains every violation node reported by those rules as an independently addressable Finding with nested minimized evidence. The user then selects one Finding at a time for LangChain-composed retrieval and evidence-sufficiency gating. Incomplete required evidence or a completed `incomplete`, `missing`, or `conflicting` retrieval ends in a terminal application-authored abstention with a clear explanation, confirmation that no provider was called, and manual-investigation guidance; it has no approve/edit/reject review decision. A retrieval execution or integrity error instead fails with no support state. An eligible Finding may produce one validated structured proposal that alone enters human review. Comparison is a separate scan-evidence path and may start from any retained baseline Finding without prior retrieval, generation, abstention, or review. The user selects one global Local-or-Groq mode for the analysis; Findings never mix providers, batch into one model call, or trigger fallback.
3. Exercise the three canonical happy-path fixture packages once with the capacity-screened local configuration and once with the exact Groq evaluation configuration. Run shared deterministic collection, valid-zero-versus-failed-scan handling, abstention, review, comparison, and the small trusted-input browser checks without duplicating them per provider. Record limitations, but do not derive statistics, provider rankings, support claims, hostile-target safety claims, or generalized quality claims.
4. Keep authenticated targets, crawling, crawler implementation, link discovery, broader rule coverage, downstream batch processing, installers, desktop packaging, signing, updates, uninstall, formal qualification, and release hardening outside the MVP. Also defer production URL-security controls—including DNS/IP filtering, egress mediation, redirect re-attestation, rebinding defenses, adversarial-page qualification, and exhaustive numeric request/resource policies—until demonstrated product need justifies a later decision.

Authorization and sequencing do not make implementation, acceptance, qualification, or release claims. A distributable product would still require the separate security, provider, application-accessibility, packaging, lifecycle, and release gates owned by their requirements modules.

## Principal risks and required mitigations

Each mitigation becomes required before the capability or claim that creates its risk enters an authorized stage; the table is not a demand to build every control in the first portfolio slice. The small portfolio controls in RSK-002, RSK-003, and RSK-006; Groq egress and credential controls in RSK-012; developer-managed local-model setup in RSK-013; the application-owned Local data boundary in RSK-014; and the narrow loopback and content-display controls in RSK-015 and RSK-016 apply to the planned MVP. RSK-007 records an explicitly accepted trusted-input limitation rather than an MVP hardening gate. Authenticated-target support, hostile or private-target containment, production URL-security controls, application-managed artifact lifecycle, concurrent replacement controls, and packaged-release controls remain deferred.

### OD-020 — Authorized public-page analysis scope

**Status:** Accepted for the MVP on 2026-08-25; authorization-attestation, hostile-target containment, and exhaustive numeric-bound clauses partially superseded by OD-021 on 2026-08-27.

OD-020 accepts the smallest public-page interaction that preserves the evidence-first portfolio goal:

- the user supplies one authorized, non-authenticated public HTTPS page, selects one global generation mode, and activates one **Analyze** action;
- one deterministic scan evaluates exactly `image-alt`, `label`, and `color-contrast` and, on success, retains every violation node from those rules as an independent finding;
- native axe `incomplete` or other scanner manual-review observations remain distinct from violations; they can coexist with a completed zero-violation scan but prevent any all-clear interpretation, while a navigation, timeout, or scan failure never looks like a valid zero-finding scan;
- after the complete finding list and its minimized per-finding evidence are available, the user selects one finding at a time for retrieval and evidence-sufficiency gating; incomplete required Finding evidence, or a completed `incomplete`, `missing`, or `conflicting` retrieval, produces a terminal application-authored abstention with explanation and manual-investigation guidance, while complete required evidence plus a completed `supported` retrieval may produce a generated proposal that alone enters human review; a retrieval execution or integrity error instead fails with no support state or abstention; comparison remains a separate scan-evidence path available from any retained baseline Finding without those downstream prerequisites;
- one explicit global `Local` or `Groq` mode applies to the analysis. Calls contain one selected eligible finding only; modes never mix within the analysis, findings never batch into one proposal, and no failure causes fallback;
- the three project-owned synthetic profiles and their failing/corrected states remain the canonical, reproducible evaluation inputs and do not enter the user-submitted runtime URL path; and
- a navigation error, finite-timeout expiry, or scan failure stops the run without publishing a completed zero-finding or silently partial result.

[OD-021](#od-021--trusted-operator-url-boundary-for-the-portfolio-mvp) and [ADR-0018](../architecture/decisions/ADR-0018-trusted-operator-url-boundary.md) replace OD-020's earlier authorization-attestation control, hostile-target containment, and exhaustive numeric-bound planning gates. Authorization and selection of an appropriate public target remain the operator's responsibility rather than a condition the application technically proves. Formal security qualification, hostile or private-target support, production URL-security controls, authenticated-page analysis, broader rule coverage, and unrestricted browser automation remain Deferred. Superseded [ADR-0017](../architecture/decisions/ADR-0017-authorized-public-page-scan-boundary.md) preserves the earlier design as decision history.

OD-020 partially supersedes only OD-018's singleton scenario/work-unit wording and OD-019's synthetic-only and one-profile-at-a-time runtime boundary. OD-018's terminal-status, no-overwrite, no-partial-publication, and no queue/concurrency/cancellation/workflow-engine decisions remain Accepted. OD-022 clarifies that starting again creates an independent run without retry lineage. OD-019's exact three rule/WCAG mappings, controlled evaluation profiles, contrast evidence and ordered comparison measure, no authentication, no broader rule coverage, and no crawling/crawler decisions also remain Accepted.

OD-006's minimization, local-retention, and deletion boundary now applies to target-derived public-page records as well as controlled evaluation runs. OD-008's fixture identity remains the deterministic evaluation correlation baseline; OD-022 accepts only exact unique rule-and-locator matching for public-page comparison and classifies missing, changed, duplicate, or ambiguous matches as `inconclusive`. OD-014 continues to exclude unrestricted browser automation; OD-021 permits only the operator-trusted page input and exact three-rule workflow recorded here.

### OD-021 — Trusted operator URL boundary for the portfolio MVP

**Status:** Accepted for the MVP on 2026-08-27.

### OD-022 — Portfolio MVP YAGNI simplification

**Status:** Accepted for the MVP on 2026-08-27.

OD-022 removes platform machinery that does not help demonstrate the first end-to-end portfolio slice:

- replace the Chroma service with LangChain's in-process `MemoryVectorStore`, EmbeddingGemma vectors, exact cosine similarity, a broad rule/success-criterion filter, and `k = 3`; application startup performs no embedding work, and the disposable collection is built on the first explicit retrieval request; Chroma is deferred until a persistent or larger corpus creates a measured need;
- require the developer to install Ollama and pull `embeddinggemma` before retrieval in either mode, and to pull `qwen3.5:4b` before Local generation; the application checks availability only when embedding or Local generation is actually requested and does not own download progress or a model-manager lifecycle;
- persist one versioned `run.json` aggregate in each run directory, nesting findings and their current downstream results; only run, finding, and corpus-passage identities are mandatory, and the MVP generates no Markdown report;
- preserve the original generated proposal and exactly one current human decision—approve, edit and accept, or reject—with its timestamp, one optional bounded note for any action, the complete edited proposal only for edit and accept, and one blocking pre-acceptance judgment disposition; keep the post-change verification reminder visible but non-blocking, and do not build actor identity, per-edit history, superseding proposal versions, or an audit workflow;
- disclose Local or Groq once when the immutable run mode is selected, keep the provider/model visibly labeled, and require an explicit Generate action for every eligible finding; use the actual provider call and returned-output validation instead of a separate connection-check or synthetic-probe subsystem;
- compare public-page findings only when page and scan profiles align and the same rule has one exact unique locator match; ambiguous, missing, or changed matches are `inconclusive`, while controlled evaluation inputs—fixture scans plus the small comparison-only policy vector—remain the deterministic source for the full comparison demonstration;
- verify the application's own accessibility with semantic implementation, automated checks, one keyboard path, and one screen-reader smoke path; participant recruitment and an exhaustive assistive-technology matrix are deferred; and
- keep the Gherkin documents non-executable and compact: one behavioral example per essential workflow concern and only the true non-negotiable hard boundaries. The fixed six generation evaluations remain three scenarios through Local and the same three through Groq, not a provider comparison.

OD-022 does not remove RAG, LangChain, local embeddings, the Local/Groq provider interface, evidence sufficiency, deterministic abstention, citations, human review, or rescan comparison. Within the earlier decision register, it narrows only OD-006's retained representation, OD-008's public-page correlation, OD-012's persistence shape, OD-014's vector-storage topology, OD-015's record-validation scope, and OD-018's retry wording. It does not narrow OD-009's fixed evaluation manifest, OD-013's fixed two-provider choice, or OD-016's deterministic corpus segmentation. Its other simplifications narrow the applicable requirements and ADR clauses for developer-managed model setup, review history, provider probing and disclosure, application-accessibility evaluation, and planning specifications.
